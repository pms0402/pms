The six 0.9.0 attack sheets were edited with the image tool from the previous original sheets, preserving tall adult proportions and removing perspective turns. They originally used four anticipation, two impact and eighteen recovery drawings. The downward plunge holds its dive drawing until terrain contact. In 0.9.1 the first cut was replaced with a larger two-handed diagonal motion and thrust received its own 24-drawing sheet with a clear elbow-back chamber. Counter uses the first cut; anti-air uses the rising sequence. Steel is registered at length 48; rendered trails and collision use the same rigid rotating samples.

In 0.9.4 the forehand, overhead finisher, charged overhead and airborne cut were regenerated with six preparation, ten distinct in-between cutting poses and eight recovery drawings. The actual swing now lasts 0.20–0.24 seconds rather than two 60 Hz frames. Runtime material palettes are matched to the original ready stance when each hero cell is cached, preventing changes in clothing exposure between sheets. All four replacements were made with the image tool from the project's own original character references.

In 0.9.5 three locomotion atlases were generated from the original repairman and body references. Walking and running share a distance-integrated foot phase and switch at contact; start/stop use dedicated body drawings. The run atlas has per-cell horizontal registration to close the loop without a body-position reset. All locomotion sheets use a single fixed anatomical scale; running flight is preserved instead of aligning every sole to the floor.

In 0.9.6 five original boss motion atlases add 180 actual drawings: walk cycles, 23 dedicated six-drawing pattern sequences and standing parry poses for the gatekeeper and saint. Chained second blows use the opposing cut sequence. The image tool repacked each atlas with empty gutters and complete weapons to remove adjacent-cell fragments. Runtime crops 192px cells, thresholds alpha and uses fixed per-sequence scale/sole registration; it does not synthesize a character from primitive shapes or distort individual body poses. The earlier boss atlases remain for story stabilization, phase transitions and death.

In 0.10.0 the two new bosses each have their own 48-cell design, including walk, five distinct attacks, death and story poses. Runtime uses 192px extraction, hard alpha, a fixed neutral-body scale per sequence and measured sole anchors. The new panoramas and facilities are generated pixel-style artwork, not photographs. Existing three normal enemy archetypes are reused at higher ranks; no new normal-enemy sprite sheet is claimed.

Original generated outputs are retained in `/workspace/generated_images/`. Runtime code crops the explicit grid, rasterizes animation cells and disables interpolation. It does not use a photographic source or apply a photo-to-pixel effect. Alpha is thresholded for crisp sprite outlines. The source sheets are generated artwork, not represented as a manually hand-pixelled commercial pack.

The score and sound effects are authored in `src/audio.js` using Web Audio oscillators. There are no sampled recordings or external audio assets. Fonts are the player's installed system fonts.

The supplied world, characters and story belong to the user's project. This provenance note identifies the actual source rather than asserting an unrelated third-party asset license or exclusive ownership of generated images.

Version 0.11.0 replaces all seven boss motion sheets and adds seven native-enemy sheets, two genuinely distinct phase-two sheets and the combat-object atlas. The originals remain PNG assets. `atlas-layout.js` stores measured source gutters and a fixed scale per atlas; `boss-registration.js` stores the measured soles and weapon endpoints. The runtime extracts cells with nearest-neighbour sampling and thresholds alpha without creating new poses. Bosses have 450 distinct motion/story drawings and the 21 ordinary species have 378; the inspection suite verifies all 828 extracted cells independently. These are image-tool-created sprite sheets, not photographs processed with a pixel filter.
